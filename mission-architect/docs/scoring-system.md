## 1. How Scoring Works

After the player submits their spacecraft design, the backend runs two independent evaluations:

1. **Feasibility checks** — Binary pass/fail checks for physical and logistical constraints (see Section 2).
2. **Score calculation** — A weighted average across scoring categories, producing an overall score from 0 to 100.

Both evaluations run every time. A design can pass scoring but fail a feasibility check, or vice versa.

---

## 2. Feasibility Checks vs Score

Feasibility checks are **not** part of the score. They are hard gates.

| Check | Type | Service |
|---|---|---|
| Mandatory subsystems all present | Feasibility (binary) | `feasibility.service.ts` |
| Mass ≤ launcher payload capacity | Feasibility (binary) | `feasibility.service.ts` |
| Power output ≥ draw × reserve factor | Feasibility (binary) | `feasibility.service.ts` |
| Battery capacity covers eclipse | Feasibility (binary) | `feasibility.service.ts` |
| Comms data rate covers instrument output | Feasibility (binary) | `feasibility.service.ts` |
| Launcher supports selected orbit | Feasibility (binary) | `compatibility.service.ts` |
| Total cost ≤ budget | ⚠️ TBD — see Section 7 | `finance.service.ts` |

These weights are a **roadmap suggestion** (not yet confirmed). All weights sum to 100.

| Scoring Category | Proposed Weight | What It Measures |
|---|---|---|
| Structure & Subsystems | 20 | Whether the core spacecraft bus and essential subsystems are appropriate for the mission |

## 4. Proposed Reward Tiers

These reward tiers translate the overall weighted score into a player reward percentage. The intent is to encourage partial credit — a player who gets most things right should still receive a meaningful reward, even if their design is not optimal.

| Overall Score | Reward Tier | Reward |
|---|---|---|
| 90–100 | Gold | 100% mission reward |
| 70–89 | Silver | 85% mission reward |
| 50–69 | Bronze | 60% mission reward |
| 30–49 | Partial | 30% mission reward |
| < 30 | Fail | 0% (mission failed — score threshold not met) |

> **Vineeth:** Confirm these tier boundaries and reward percentages. Implement in `scoring.service.ts` or a results service. Do not expose tier boundaries in any frontend data file.

## 5. Pass/Fail Threshold

This rule is **agreed** (not just proposed) and is already documented in `docs/game-design.md` and `docs/missions/earth-observation-01.md`:

- Overall weighted score **< 30** → mission fails the score threshold.
- Overall weighted score **≥ 30** → passes the score threshold.
- A score of exactly **30** is a valid passing grade.

A design that passes the score threshold can still fail a feasibility check. Both must be communicated to the player on the Results page.

## 6. How Category Scores Are Generated

Category scores are computed by the backend (`scoring.service.ts` and `evaluation.service.ts`). The exact algorithms are for Vineeth to implement. The following guidance describes the *intent* of each category's score, not the implementation.

### Structure & Subsystems (weight: 20)
Score reflects whether the structural bus and mandatory non-instrument subsystems are present and form a coherent, well-balanced spacecraft. A complete design with well-matched mass and power budgets should score highly.

### Scientific Instruments (weight: 25)
Score reflects how well the selected instrument(s) match the mission category (Earth observation) and how much data they produce. Selecting instruments not designed for Earth observation should score zero in this category.

### Power System (weight: 15)
Score reflects how well-sized the solar + battery combination is. A system that barely meets the power reserve requirement scores lower than one with good headroom. An overpowered system does not necessarily score higher — good engineering is about matching, not over-specifying.

### Communications (weight: 10)
Score reflects whether the communications system can comfortably handle the data rate produced by the instruments. A design where the antenna is significantly under-capacity scores low even if it technically passes the feasibility check (marginal designs should not score as well as well-matched ones).

### Orbit Selection (weight: 15)
Score reflects how appropriate the selected orbit is for Earth observation. Sun-synchronous orbit scores higher for consistent imagery missions. LEO at 500 km is acceptable and scores reasonably. An orbit that is not in `allowedOrbitIds` should score zero.

### Launch Vehicle (weight: 15)
Score reflects how well the launcher matches the payload. Using a large launcher for a tiny spacecraft (gross overmatch) or a launcher at the very limit of its capacity (marginal) should both score lower than a well-matched selection.
